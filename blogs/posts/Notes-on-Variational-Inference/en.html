Why Learn Variational Inference

In probabilistic modeling, we often face the following situation: given observed data x, we wish to infer the posterior distribution of the latent variable z, written as \(p(z \mid x)\). Unless the model is simple, this distribution is usually intractable because of the marginal likelihood in the denominator:

\[ p(x) = \int p(x, z)\,dz \]

In high-dimensional spaces, this integral becomes almost impossible to compute. Instead of insisting on the exact posterior, we introduce an approximate distribution \(q_\phi(z)\) that is easier to handle, and try to make it close to the true \(p_\theta(z \mid x)\). Variational Inference (VI) formalizes this idea.

From KL Divergence to the Lower Bound

We aim to find parameters \(\phi\) that minimize the difference between these two distributions, expressed as minimizing the Kullback–Leibler divergence:

\[ \mathrm{KL}\bigl(q_\phi(z)\,\|\,p_\theta(z \mid x)\bigr) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z)}\!\left[\log\frac{q_\phi(z)}{p_\theta(z\mid x)}\right]. \]

Expanding the conditional probability \(p_\theta(z \mid x) = \frac{p_\theta(x, z)}{p_\theta(x)}\) gives:

\[ \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z)\,\|\,p_\theta(z \mid x)) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z)}[\log q_\phi(z) - \log p_\theta(x, z)] + \log p_\theta(x). \]

Since the KL divergence is non-negative, we can rearrange terms and obtain:

\[ \log p_\theta(x) \ge \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z)}[\log p_\theta(x, z) - \log q_\phi(z)]. \]

The right-hand side is the well-known Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO):

\[ \mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi; x) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z)\,\|\,p_\theta(z)). \]

Maximizing this lower bound is equivalent to minimizing the KL divergence between the approximate and the true posterior. This is why the term “variational lower bound” exists.

Intuition: The Meaning of the Bound

From the perspective of graphical models, the ELBO can be viewed as an optimizable surrogate of the log-likelihood:

  • The first term \(\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]\) measures how well the model reconstructs the data;
  • The second term \(-\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi\|p_\theta)\) penalizes approximations that deviate too far from the prior.

In other words, it balances two forces: the ability to explain data and the discipline to stay close to prior beliefs.

In models like the Variational Autoencoder (VAE), both \(q_\phi(z\mid x)\) and \(p_\theta(x\mid z)\) are represented by differentiable networks. Through the reparameterization trick, sampling becomes differentiable, enabling gradient-based optimization of the ELBO:

\[ z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x)\odot \epsilon, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I). \]

Approximation, Not Compromise

As the derivation shows, the ELBO is not an arbitrary surrogate; it is a mathematically exact lower bound—representing the portion of the true likelihood that we can actually reach. It reminds me that inference is not about chasing exactness, but about making an honest approximation within computational limits.

Whenever we adjust the model, choose a variational family, or reformulate the bound, we are implicitly redefining how much of the world our model is allowed to understand.
